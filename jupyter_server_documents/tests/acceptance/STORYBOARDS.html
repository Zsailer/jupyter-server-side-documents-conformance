Notebooks in more than one tab: what Jupyter does when things go wrong

69 stories about one notebook that is open in one or more browser tabs while connections drop, the server lets go of the notebook or dies, or the file changes underneath. Each is drawn step by step and ends with what every copy of the notebook must hold. This page says what the product should do, whatever the code does today. Where that is not settled, the story is marked “Needs alignment” and shows the proposed answer next to the alternatives. The last group, 9 stories, is about AI agents such as Claude Code: it is new, and no scenario of the acceptance suite plays those stories yet.

Coming back is not specialA tab that returns after being offline is treated like someone who stayed online: its work is merged with everyone else's by the same rules, and nothing is replaced wholesale.

A dropped connection, a lost message, a rebuilt notebook, a moved cell or a missing cell id never make it disappear and never show a cell twice. Nobody is cut off for being slowA tab that answers late is still served. A stray message from a tab that is gone never stops the notebook for the people who are still there.

Where we need to align

11 of the stories do not have a settled answer yet. Each shows, below its picture, the proposed behavior and the alternatives. The proposals follow one rule of thumb: where two people's intentions conflict (a deletion against typing, two rewrites of one text) the merge decides, as it does between two people who are online; where the machinery could lose or duplicate something that nobody meant to, nothing may be lost or duplicated. The answers marked “Suggested” belong to the stories about AI agents: they are new, and nobody has discussed them yet.
